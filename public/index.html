How these numbers are measured

Down is time covered by failed availability checks: a timeout, an error response, a refused connection or missing page content. Each check counts until the next one, and never longer than 15 minutes. With checks every 5 to 6 minutes, a single failed check adds about 5 minutes, so short or intermittent failures are estimates accurate to one check interval.

Sustained downtime needs two or more failed checks in a row. Slow means the page answered but took longer than its target. Not checked is time with no fresh check, for example when GitHub delayed the scheduled monitor; it is never counted as up or down. Uptime is up time divided by checked time.

Checks run on GitHub's public servers in US data centers and reach EasyChamp through the nearest Cloudflare location. A problem limited to one region, such as Miami or Atlanta, can be missed or show up only as occasional failures.

Alerts: an issue opens when two of the latest four checks of a service fail within 30 minutes, or when two consecutive complete checks fail. It closes after six checks in a row pass. Unknown results never open or close an issue.

Operational means the configured checks passed. Signed-in workflows, video and most background data are covered only where a dedicated check exists; open a service for its exact scope.
